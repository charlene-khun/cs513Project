This project focused on improving the quality and usability of a large historical restaurant menu dataset containing information about menus, dishes, menu pages, and menu items. In a business or analytics context, inconsistent, incomplete, and invalid data can prevent analysts from making reliable comparisons or identifying meaningful trends. Our team addressed this problem by profiling the dataset, identifying data-quality issues, and cleaning important attributes such as menu currency and menu-item prices. We used tools including OpenRefine and SQL to transform the raw data into a more consistent and analysis-ready form while documenting the changes made throughout the cleaning process.

The primary stakeholders for this project were researchers, data analysts, and organizations interested in using historical menu data to study topics such as food pricing and changes in restaurant offerings over time. Because fields such as dish names and other descriptive attributes contained significant inconsistencies, the project focused on a use case where reliable numerical price information could provide more meaningful analysis. We developed data-quality rules and integrity checks to determine which records contained usable price information and validated relationships among key tables such as Menu, MenuPage, Dish, and MenuItem. This helped ensure that downstream users could work with a more dependable subset of the original data rather than making decisions based on invalid or poorly structured records.

The cleaned dataset supports decisions about whether historical menu records are sufficiently reliable for quantitative analysis, particularly when comparing prices across menus or time periods. By identifying usable records, enforcing consistency rules, and documenting data transformations and lineage, the project demonstrates how data curation directly affects the reliability of analytical results. The broader significance is that even a large and potentially valuable dataset has limited business or research value if users cannot trust its quality. By improving data consistency and making the cleaning process transparent and reproducible, the project created a stronger foundation for future analysis and more defensible data-driven conclusions.
